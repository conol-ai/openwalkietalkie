OpenWalkieTalkie 0.12.0 lets Conol transcribe what you say to its chats with its own speech model, shows a recording timeline on the deck while you talk, mirrors Conol's current activity, and adds a Timer face with a stopwatch, a countdown and Pomodoro.

## Conol hears you directly

When you hold PTT on a Conol chat or **+ New chat**, the Mac service now records the press and hands it to the Conol desktop app, which transcribes it with Conol's cloud speech model, the same one behind the microphone in Conol's composer. Conol's text is what the deck shows after release and what is sent, including dictated answers to Conol questions.

The Mac's own recognizer (Apple, or Whisper if you installed it) still runs during the press. If Conol can't answer, fails, or takes longer than 20 seconds, the Mac's transcript is used instead, so a press is never lost. Other agents and naming the deck are unchanged and stay on your Mac. Only presses addressed to Conol chats leave the Mac, and only to the Conol app you are signed in to. To keep all recognition local, set `[voice.stt] companion = false`.

This needs the updated Conol desktop app and Conol web version that support deck transcription. With an older Conol app, presses are transcribed on the Mac exactly as before.

## A recording timeline instead of a guess

For those presses the deck no longer shows the Mac's live words, which are not what Conol will hear. It shows **RECORDING**, a large elapsed-time readout and a timeline: a red playhead moves left to right while the waveform of your voice, taken from the deck's own microphone, fills in behind it. Longer presses scroll. After release the deck shows **TRANSCRIBING**, then Conol's transcript.

## See what Conol is doing

A running Conol chat now shows its current public step on the deck, for example "Running a command" with a short preview, in the roster and in the conversation. It updates in place and clears when the step completes or is stopped. Full tool inputs and outputs stay in Conol.

## Timer and Pomodoro

Add **Timer** from the face manager's **+** gallery. It has a stopwatch with laps, a countdown with presets, and **Pomodoro**: 25 minutes of focus and 5-minute breaks, with a 15-minute break after four focus sessions. Each phase waits for **Next / Enter**. The timers run on the deck alone, keep running on other pages and while the screen sleeps, and wake the screen with a toast when they finish.

## For companion developers

Native companions can register `caps.transcribe: "push"` to receive `agent.transcribe` offers (16 kHz WAV), fetch them with `agent.transcribe.audio` and answer with `agent.transcribe.result`. `service.info` lists `transcribe.companion`. Activity rows use `agent.reply` with `message.summary`. See the [native companion guide](https://openwalkietalkie.org/docs/native-companions/) and the [API reference](https://openwalkietalkie.org/docs/deckd-api.md).

## Update

Choose **Check for Updates**, or install the signed, notarized **0.12.0** Mac app for Apple Silicon or Intel. macOS 13 or later is required. If the service still reports an older version, choose **Service → Restart**.

Firmware **0.12.0** is required for the recording timeline and the Timer face. With the deck connected by USB, open **Service → Updates → Check firmware**. Settings and Bluetooth pairing are preserved. Factory images are for manufacturing and overwrite settings and pairing.

## Validation

On a Rev B deck with a local Conol build, Conol answered all five test presses (3 to 15 seconds of speech) 2.6 to 4.6 seconds after release. Tests cover offers reaching only the owning app, paged audio, first-answer-wins, immediate fallback on failure or disconnect, the 20-second budget, the recording marker replacing live words, and the timeline's layout, scrolling and scaling. The Mac workspace, the firmware for all three boards and Clippy passed locally. The release pipeline repeats these checks before signing and notarizing both Mac packages.
